How it works

Four questions a seller can answer before it ships.

One NEAR contract holds the mandate, every agent under it, the money locked for each order, and the rule for who pays. GLEIF’s August 2026 discussion paper calls for an Agent Mandate Credential; ours is a proposed schema.

Who answers

A known guarantor, a sealed ledger

The guarantor checks the company’s legal identity off-chain, here with GLEIF’s vLEI credentials, and signs. It is sealed from the public ledger, which would otherwise show what the company buys and from whom. A seller who needs the name for an invoice can get it off-chain. In this demo the issuers are mocks.

Who pays

A fixed order, written in advance

On default: the agent’s vault, then the company’s vault including its bond, then the guarantor’s stake. The seller claims, no approval needed; the company can stop it only by escrowing the full amount and disputing. It then has a recourse period to repay the guarantor and stay sealed.

What it may do

Only ever narrower

An agent hires by spending budget links the employee already released. Payees, expiry, rate and per-payment cap can only shrink, up to 3 levels and 16 agents. Every payment is checked on-chain: payee, unpaid invoice, cap, hash-chain budget, rate, expiry.

When it ends

Stop-ship, for every seller

NEAR lets a contract add and delete keys on its own account, and each agent key is restricted by the protocol to five methods. If a debt is never repaid, closing the root deletes every key in one batch, up to 16 agents, so the company’s agents stop buying from every seller at once. Credit insurers ask each seller to stop shipping on its own. Money left in the tree repays the guarantor first.

Security

The guarantee only works if a fooled agent can’t commit the company beyond its mandate.

So everything below is live: try to steal from an agent, fool a real model, or fire an employee.

    All known limits
    • The vLEI schemas are GLEIF’s; the issuers are mocks with a fictional LEI, and no real QVI issued anything. The Agent Mandate Credential schema is a proposal, since GLEIF has not published one.
    • Testnet only, no real users, and no real guarantor has underwritten anything. The fee here is a demo number: 2%. Who would guarantee an agent, and at what price, is an open commercial question. The selling story here runs in Circle’s testnet USDC; the terms text is a placeholder no lawyer has reviewed.
    • A guarantor that also holds the company’s legal-entity key could name that company falsely: the contract checks the signature, and the link between that key and the LEI is checked off-chain.
    • This page’s config holds the fictional company’s LEI and salt so the guarantor’s step runs in your browser; a real guarantor keeps them private.
    • The company’s LEI stays a commitment until it is disclosed, but funding accounts and payments are public and linkable.
    • Revocation takes effect when the signed event reaches the contract; no relayer ships with the repo. A fooled payment inside every limit, to a permitted payee, for a new invoice, still gets paid.
    • Disclosure takes the guarantor’s action, since it holds the salt, and it does not happen if money left in the tree repays the debt. Closing affects the agents under one mandate, not every mandate the company has.
    • The buttons on this page sign real testnet transactions, with short windows so you can watch them: 15 seconds to due, 20 seconds of recourse. The guarantor’s steps run from this page.
