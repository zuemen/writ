    How it works

    Four questions a seller can answer before it ships.

    GLEIF's August 2026 discussion paper calls for an Agent Mandate Credential and leaves sub-delegation between agents open. Writ puts the mandate's state, the whole tree under it, and who pays for its debts in one NEAR contract.

    Who answers

    A sealed name, a named guarantor

    Root → QVI → legal entity → employee on the official vLEI schemas, then a proposed Agent Mandate Credential to the agent. The guarantor checks the chain off-chain with keripy and signs. On-chain there is only a commitment to the LEI. The issuers in this demo are mocks.

    Who pays

    A fixed order, written in advance

    On default: the agent's vault, then the company's vault including its bond, then the guarantor's stake. The seller claims, no approval needed; the company can stop it only by escrowing the full amount and disputing. It then has a recourse period to repay the guarantor and stay sealed.

    What it may do

    Only ever narrower

    An agent hires by spending budget links the employee already released. Payees, expiry, rate and per-payment cap can only shrink, up to 3 levels and 16 agents. Every payment is checked on-chain: payee, unpaid invoice, cap, hash-chain budget, rate, expiry.

    When it ends

    The key stops existing

    NEAR lets a contract add and delete keys on its own account, and each agent key is restricted by the protocol to five methods. Closing the root deletes every key in one batch: 16 agents, 17 Tgas. Debts keep the money in the tree until they are paid.

    • The vLEI schemas are GLEIF's; the issuers are mocks with a fictional LEI, and no real QVI issued anything. The Agent Mandate Credential schema is a proposal, since GLEIF has not published one.
    • Testnet only, no real users, and no real guarantor has underwritten anything. The fee here is a demo number: 2%. Who would guarantee an agent, and at what price, is an open commercial question. Credit is in NEAR only; the terms text is a placeholder no lawyer has reviewed.
    • A guarantor that also holds the company's legal-entity key could name that company falsely: the contract checks the signature, and the link between that key and the LEI is checked off-chain.
    • This page's config holds the fictional company's LEI and salt so the guarantor's step runs in your browser; a real guarantor keeps them private.
    • The company's LEI stays a commitment until it is disclosed, but funding accounts and payments are public and linkable.
    • Revocation takes effect when the signed event reaches the contract; no relayer ships with the repo. A fooled payment inside every limit, to a permitted payee, for a new invoice, still gets paid.
    • Disclosure takes the guarantor's action, since it holds the salt, and it does not happen if money left in the tree repays the debt. Closing affects the agents under one mandate, not every mandate the company has.
