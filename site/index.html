    Why the key is not enough

    1. The key decides who can act. NEAR restricts it at the protocol level to one contract and one method, spend.
    2. The mandate decides how much, and to whom. Payees and limits come from a credential a verifier checked. The only allowed payee here is .
    3. A hash chain decides whether it may spend now. The budget is a chain of preimages held by the employee, released link by link. Without the next one, the key has nothing to spend.
    4. HR can take it all back. On other cells, HR revokes the employee's credential and the contract deletes the agent's key. That part is in the testnet run.

    This cell keeps its key after repeated failures so that you can keep trying; a normal mandate deletes the key after three rejections in a row. Each attempt uses some of the key's gas allowance, so the page may report NotEnoughAllowance until the funder refills it. Writ limits what a stolen key or a fooled agent can do. It cannot stop a holder of both the key and the employee's links.
