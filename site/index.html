    Who pays is settled before the agent spends.

    Before

    A guarantor is named

    It signs the mandate, takes its fee and locks the full limit in its stake. It can’t withdraw that lock while cover runs.

    While it spends

    Every payment is recorded

    The contract checks payee, invoice, cap, budget, rate and expiry, and keeps a hash of what the agent read and decided.

    When it’s fooled

    The arbiter decides

    The company files a claim and its whole tree stops. The contract can’t tell if the agent was fooled, so a named arbiter rules. The limit caps what a wrong ruling costs.

    After

    The stake repays

    The loss, minus the company’s share, moves from the stake to the company. The tree stays stopped until the company clears its agent.

    The guarantor checks who the company is off-chain, with GLEIF’s vLEI credentials and KERI; here the issuers are mocks. NEAR lets the contract hold agent keys and delete them, so a revoked agent’s transactions never get created.

    Same stake, second use: net-30 for sellers.

    The guarantor’s stake can also back an order on credit. You don’t need to know the company; the guarantor does, and it approved your account in the mandate first. Set a rule. If the buyer doesn’t pay, claim it, with no one’s approval.

    • Testnet only, no real users, and no real guarantor has underwritten anything. Whether this is insurance is unknown, so it stays on testnet.
    • The arbiter is someone you must trust. Bought by the company, it costs the guarantor up to the limit. Bought by the guarantor, it can end your cover.
    • Not audited. The vLEI issuers are mocks with a fictional LEI.
    All known limits
    • Moral hazard is capped, not stopped. A company that fools its own agent, with a seller it controls, can take the loss minus its share, up to the limit, if the arbiter upholds.
    • Seller refunds are invisible. If a seller refunds a fooled payment, the contract can’t see it, so a company could be repaid twice. The terms ask for a statement; nothing on-chain checks it.
    • No company-wide total. Each mandate has its own limit. Nothing adds up a company’s mandates or a guarantor’s view of them.
    • A guarantor can revoke early and keep the fee. The fee is charged once, when the guarantor chooses to activate in the first half of the term, and never refunded. Its lock stays until the mandate’s term and claim window have passed.
    • Whether this is insurance is unknown. We don’t claim to be a licensed product. It depends on the jurisdiction, and no lawyer has reviewed it. A guarantor would need its own licence or legal opinion. No real users, testnet only.
    • A fooled payment inside every limit, to a permitted payee, for a new invoice, still gets paid. Cover repays it afterwards, up to the limit, if the arbiter upholds.
    • This page holds the company’s and the arbiter’s restricted keys, so any visitor can file, rule and clear. Money only moves to the company’s account. A real mandate has a real arbiter.
    • The demo’s arbiter has 60 seconds and a payment can be claimed for 10 minutes; the contract’s default for the arbiter is 14 days. An open claim stops the agent for every visitor, and the budget has a low daily rate, so the page can run out for a day. The page’s keys spend a gas allowance anyone can burn, and it says so when one runs out.
    • The model is on a free tier: the quota can run out, and the page says so. Payment answers to the same invoice text are cached for a day, and the page says when.
    • The vLEI schemas are GLEIF’s; the issuers are mocks with a fictional LEI, and no real QVI issued anything. The Agent Mandate Credential schema is a proposal, since GLEIF has not published one.
    • The fee here is a demo number. Who would guarantee an agent, and at what price, is an open commercial question. The terms text is a placeholder no lawyer has reviewed.
    • A guarantor that also holds the company’s legal-entity key could name that company falsely: the contract checks the signature, and the link between that key and the LEI is checked off-chain.
    • This page’s config holds the fictional company’s LEI and salt so the guarantor’s step runs in your browser; a real guarantor keeps them private.
    • The company’s LEI stays a commitment until it is disclosed, but funding accounts and payments are public and linkable.
    • Revocation takes effect when the signed event reaches the contract; no relayer ships with the repo.
    • Disclosure takes the guarantor’s action, since it holds the salt, and it does not happen if money left in the tree repays the debt. Closing affects the agents under one mandate, not every mandate the company has.
    • The buttons on this page sign real testnet transactions, with short windows so you can watch them: 15 seconds to due, 20 seconds of recourse. The guarantor’s steps run from this page. In the net-30 and firing stories the agents are scripted; only “Fool a real model” calls a model.

    The threat model has the rest. make verify reruns the build, the bench, the stories and the credential checks, and prints a scorecard.
